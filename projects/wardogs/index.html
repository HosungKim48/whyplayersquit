WARDOGS: The complaints that don't make players leave — and the ones that do

BULKHEAD / Team17 · large-scale PvP tactical shooter · Steam app 1867240 · Early Access since Sep 10, 2026
Live report, refreshed weekly · this version Nov 20, 2026 · data through Nov 20, 2026 · tracked since Nov 19, 2026
Analysis by Hosung Kim

Key findings

The most common complaints in WARDOGS' negative reviews, cheaters and balance, are not the ones that come before players quit; the 175 ms ping cap, season progression wipes and trust in bans are.

Details
  • The ping cap is the strongest quit signal. Among 10–50h players whose main complaint was the cap, 70% (n = 713) stopped playing, against 35% (n = 8,470) for all 10–50h reviewers.
  • Season 2 nearly doubled quitting among 10–50h players: from 31.7% (n = 1,040) in the two weeks before it to 60.4% (n = 2,364) in the two weeks after.
  • Cheaters are loud, not decisive. They are the most common main complaint, 15.5% (n = 56,407) of negative reviews, yet 10–50h players citing them stopped at 28% (n = 1,323), below the 35% (n = 8,470) for all 10–50h reviewers.

What we'd suggest

  1. 1Shorten time-to-fun for new players

    Evidence
    88% (n = 661) of 0–2h reviewers whose main complaint was gameplay & design stopped playing, against 54% (n = 3,830) for the whole 0–2h group.
    Who · what to watch · ideas
    Player group
    0–2h and 2–10h.
    Metric to watch
    stop rate of 0–2h reviewers whose main complaint is gameplay & design — now 88% (n = 661).
    What it could look like
    faster routes to the action, shorter respawn-to-fight time, a clearer first-hour loop. Queue fixes help sentiment, but queue complainers mostly stayed.

  2. 2Protect 10–50h players at season resets

    Evidence
    47% (n = 662) of 10–50h reviewers whose main complaint was a progression wipe stopped playing, against 35% (n = 8,470) for the whole 10–50h group.
    Who · what to watch · ideas
    Player group
    10–50h.
    Metric to watch
    10–50h stop rate in the two weeks after a season starts — after Season 2 it was 60.4% (n = 2,364) — and whether the Season 3 catch-up mechanic brings it down.
    What it could look like
    carry over part of the progress, add catch-up bonuses, publish reset rules well before a season starts.

  3. 3Post policy changes on Steam as well as X

    Evidence
    67.1% (n = 5,321) of reviews whose main complaint was the ping cap were written in a language other than English. The 175 ms limit was announced in one English post on X, and no Steam announcement covered it.
    Who · what to watch · ideas
    Player group
    players outside the server's home region, at every playtime.
    Metric to watch
    share of negative reviews citing a policy change in the week it takes effect — for the 175 ms limit, 34% (n = 9,041).
    What it could look like
    a Steam announcement for every policy change, which reaches every owner in their library; an estimate of how many active players each region would lose before a region-sensitive change; a note to players who stopped during the cap period about the 250 ms limit.

Notes

The most common main reason in each group:

  • 0–2h: servers & access, 31.5% (n = 4,437), mostly launch queues.
  • 2–10h: gameplay & design, 21.6% (n = 6,106): long travel, waiting, dying unseen.
  • 10–50h: grind & gear gap, 18.3% (n = 13,646).
  • 50–200h: cheaters, 19.2% (n = 22,578).
  • 200h+: cheaters, 20.4% (n = 9,640), then balance, with dev trust & bans rising.

Common is not the same as decisive. The table below shows the stop rate among reviewers whose main reason is each row. * = fewer than 100 reviewers. Switch to “Any mention” to see the same pattern using main or secondary reasons.

What stands out:

  • New players (0–10h) quit over the experience itself. Gameplay & design and grind run far above the group rate. Paraphrased: "Spawn far away, ride to the front, get sniped, wait for a revive that never comes, repeat."
  • Queue complaints did not go with quitting. Servers & access has the lowest or near-lowest stop rate in every group.
  • Invested players (10h+) quit over the ping cap, wipes, and bans. All three sit above the group rate at 10–50h and 50–200h. Among 50–200h players whose main complaint was bans or developer trust, 45% (n = 538) stopped.
  • For 10h+ players, cheaters and balance are complaints, not exits. Both sit below the group rate. At 0–10h, cheaters run slightly above it (the 0–2h cell is small). One 200h+ player, paraphrased: "Would be incredible if cheating weren't out of control; community servers are still fun with active reporting."

Season 3 effect on quitting: available Dec 3

Whether a reviewer kept playing can only be judged once the review is 14 days old. On Dec 3, 2026 the stop rate for reviews written Nov 12–18 can be compared with Nov 5–11, and this box fills in automatically.

Notes
  • Oct 15 — Season 2 (Steam: SEASON 02 IS NOW LIVE). The Season 02 Patch Notes also introduced region-based ping limits. In its first week, wipes were the main reason in 1,118 negative reviews.
  • Oct 21–22 — 175 ms ping limit, announced on X (@WARDOGS, Oct 21) and in effect Oct 22; there was no Steam announcement. That week, the cap was the main reason in 34% (n = 9,041) of negative reviews.
  • Nov 6 — Road to Season 3 (Steam). BULKHEAD's devlog covered cheating and enforcement; the Season 03 Patch Notes put the ban wave at 185,000+ players.
  • Nov 12 — Season 3 (Steam: SEASON 03 IS NOW LIVE). The ping limit rose to 250 ms on all official servers and a catch-up mechanic arrived; wipe complaints rose again.
  • Nov 19 — Patch 0.31 (Steam: Minor Balance Patch 0.31). Lower vendor prices and tank unlock levels; too recent to show in this data.

What Season 2 did to 10–50h players. Their stop rate nearly doubled:

  • Before (Oct 1–14): 31.7% (n = 1,040).
  • After (Oct 15–28): 60.4% (n = 2,364).
  • After, excluding the Oct 22–24 review spike: 59.7% (n = 1,545), so this is not just a three-day surge.

Two paraphrases from this group:

  • "Season 2 reset all my progress to zero with nothing to show; I don't support games that waste my time."
  • "I was enjoying NA servers with good people, then suddenly got dragged out and locked away."

The cap acted as involuntary churn. Many of these players were cut off from the servers they played on rather than tired of the game. By definition, "stopped" reviewers had not played since shortly after their review as of data collection on Nov 20, a week after the limit rose. That doesn't mean they won't return, only that they hadn't yet.

What changed after Season 3

Credit where due: after Season 3 raised the ping limit to 250 ms on Nov 12, ping-cap complaints fell from 19.3% (n = 21,748) of negative reviews during Season 2 to 3.0% (n = 4,783) since Season 3 began. BULKHEAD's patch notes tie the change to the ban wave and a check of regional data. Season 3 also added a catch-up mechanic for players who fall behind.

Methods & limits

Method & limits · reviewers only · correlation, not causation
  • Data: 56,407 negative Steam reviews from Steam's public review API, Sep 10 – Nov 20, 2026.
  • Languages: all 29 present, analyzed together.
  • Labels: main and secondary reasons labeled by Claude Haiku 5.5 with a fixed codebook.
  • Label accuracy: about 88% (n = 50) correct in a random spot check, so percentages carry some error.
  • n: the number of reviews, or judged reviewers, a percentage is out of.
  • Quit status: 48,102 reviews written Sep 10 – Nov 06 were old enough to judge; Season 3 reviews are not included yet.
  • Two stop-rate measures: the main text uses stopped ÷ (stopped + kept playing). The alternative, stopped ÷ all judged reviewers, also counts players in between and gives lower figures with the same pattern; on it, the 10–50h rate more than doubled after Season 2, to 43.5% (n = 3,282).
  • Event window: the Oct 22–24 review spike was partly coordinated; excluding it does not change the main findings (try “Exclude Oct 22–24” on any chart).
  • Timeline sources: official Steam announcements and BULKHEAD's own X account, linked above.
  • Correlation, not causation. A reason's stop rate shows association only.
  • Who is represented: only people who wrote negative reviews.
  • Quotes are short paraphrases chosen to illustrate findings; they are not counted.
  • Independent: not affiliated with BULKHEAD or Team17.
