How the numbers are made

Code does the cheap work first; a model only sees what survives. Every count below comes from the WARDOGS run of Nov 19, 2026.

Of the labeled reviews, 47,875 (written Sep 10 – Nov 05) were at least 14 days old at collection, old enough to judge whether the reviewer kept playing.

How much to trust the labels

78%pilot: main reason matched my hand labels (n=50)
88%pilot: matched on main or secondary reason
~88%random spot check judged correct (44 of 50)
$3.50cost to label all 56,187 reviews
  • Codebook first. I hand-read 300 negative reviews across languages, periods and playtime groups, then wrote 12 categories with include/exclude and tie-break rules. Read the codebook.
  • Pilot. 50 reviews from that hand-read set, at least four per category, labeled by the model and compared with my labels.
  • Full run. Claude Haiku 5.5 through the Message Batches API, with the codebook cached and JSON output. Each review is read in its original language.
  • Spot check. 50 random labeled reviews (none from the hand-read set), judged by me. Percentages on this site carry that error; I treat only large gaps as findings.
  • Cleaning rules. A secondary reason of “unclear / other”, or one equal to the main reason, counts as none.
  • Report text. The first draft was written by Claude Sonnet 5.5 from the analysis tables only (no raw reviews), then edited by me. All numbers are computed by code.

Did the reviewer keep playing?

Steam reports hours played at the time of the review and hours played as of collection (Nov 19, 2026). The difference sorts each reviewer into one group:

GroupRule
StoppedUnder 1 hour played after the review, and last played within 3 days of it
Kept playing10+ hours played after the review
Kept playing a little1–10 hours after; not used in stop rates
UnclearUnder 1 hour after, but last played more than 3 days after the review
Too recentReview less than 14 days old at collection; not judged (the 14-day rule)

Among judged reviews, 9,744 reviewers stopped and 23,568 kept playing.

Two stop-rate definitions

  • Stop rate B (used in the reports): stopped ÷ (stopped + kept playing). For 10–50h reviewers: 2,957 of 8,440, or 35%.
  • Stop rate A: stopped ÷ all judged reviewers, including “a little”, “unclear” and so on. For 10–50h reviewers: 2,957 of 12,582, or 23.5%. Lower numbers, same pattern.

Lift (home page chart) compares a reason's stop rate B with what you would expect from its reviewers' playtime groups alone. Comparing within playtime matters because stopping falls steeply with hours played (54% at 0–2h, 8% at 200h+).

Events and the Oct 22–24 spike

Event dates come from official Steam posts, except where press is noted: Season 2 live Oct 15; 175 ms ping cap announced on X Oct 21 (press), in effect Oct 22; ban wave of 185,000+ accounts around Nov 6 (press); Season 3 live Nov 12 with the ping limit raised to 250 ms. The Oct 22–24 review spike was a real reaction to the cap plus a partly coordinated pile-on, so every chart can leave those three days out.

Limits

  • Represents only people who wrote negative reviews, not all players.
  • Correlation, not causation. A reason's stop rate shows association only.
  • “Stopped” is a snapshot as of Nov 19; some players may return.
  • Older reviews had more time to build up playtime afterward, so “kept playing” is slightly inflated for early reviews.
  • Quotes are short paraphrases chosen to illustrate findings. No reviewer IDs or profiles are ever published.
